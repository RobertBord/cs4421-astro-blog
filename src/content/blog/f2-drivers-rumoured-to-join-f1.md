An empty Formula 1 seat attracts two things: genuine contenders and a much longer list of predictions. In May 2024, two Formula 2 drivers had particularly clear links to the conversation about the 2025 grid: Andrea Kimi Antonelli and Oliver Bearman.

This is a **historical snapshot**, published retrospectively. It assesses information available by **31 May 2024**, not the current driver market. Later contracts and championship results are deliberately outside its scope.

## Andrea Kimi Antonelli: the Mercedes question

**Confirmed at the time:** Antonelli was a Mercedes-backed junior competing for Prema in Formula 2 after skipping Formula 3. Lewis Hamilton's impending departure left Mercedes with a decision about its future lineup.

In [Lawrence Barretto's 4 May 2024 report for Formula1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/who-is-andrea-kimi-antonelli-and-why-is-he-such-a-hot-topic-in-the-miami.180WZSINYti3GYoz7hOBe7), Antonelli's development and potential route to Formula 1 were already a major paddock topic. The report described an FIA request for permission to race before his eighteenth birthday. Williams was *believed* to have made it; that attribution was not a confirmed announcement.

**The rumour:** A Williams opportunity was being discussed as a possible early route into F1, alongside the larger question of Mercedes' succession plan. The same report carried James Vowles' explicit rejection of an Imola debut.

**Our reading:** A junior programme and an opening at its parent team create a plausible path, not a signed contract. A proposed early debut also raises a separate eligibility question. Neither should be collapsed into the headline "Mercedes seat secured".

## Oliver Bearman: an audition, not an entitlement

**Confirmed at the time:** Bearman had already raced in F1, finishing seventh for Ferrari in Saudi Arabia. The next question was a full-time seat, not whether he could make a first appearance.

[Formula1.com's 16 May 2024 report](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/bearman-wants-to-be-back-in-f1-as-quickly-as-possible-as-he-prepares-for.7uJPEhoHwj48E8h7PWxsX6) identified him as a Ferrari and Haas reserve with six Haas practice outings scheduled during 2024. It also discussed the unconfirmed Haas lineup for 2025.

**The rumour:** Haas was a credible potential destination. Bearman himself acknowledged that a vacant seat did not entitle him to it. His scheduled practice work was real; a full-time 2025 agreement was not established by that report.

**Our reading:** Practice appearances let a team evaluate communication, preparation, and execution. They are stronger evidence of a working relationship than a social-media prediction, but they are still not a race contract.

## Other prospects are not automatically seat rumours

Gabriel Bortoleto and Isack Hadjar belonged in a conversation about the junior pipeline. They should not be described as confirmed transfer targets without a source saying so.

- **Bortoleto:** [McLaren's 5 October 2023 announcement](https://www.mclaren.com/racing/formula-1/2023/mclaren-announce-fia-formula-3-champion-gabriel-bortoleto-as-the-latest-driver-to-join-the-driver-development-programme/) confirmed that the 2023 F3 champion had joined its Driver Development programme. That is not evidence of a McLaren race-seat offer.
- **Hadjar:** [Red Bull Junior Team's 26 May 2024 Monaco report](https://www.redbull.com/int-en/juniorteam/monaco-f2-victory-stolen-from-hadjar-by-last-gasp-virtual-safety) documented his Campos F2 performance and his place in Red Bull's junior programme. It did not announce promotion to an F1 seat.

## What would turn the speculation into news?

A dated team announcement is the decisive change. Before that, the useful questions are narrower: who is reporting the link, what is on the record, which seat is actually available, and which eligibility or development decisions remain unresolved?

At this cutoff, Antonelli and Bearman had identifiable reasons to feature in the 2025 conversation. The responsible conclusion was that their routes were worth following, not that either destination was guaranteed.
